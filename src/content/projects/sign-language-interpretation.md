This survey paper was conducted as part of the research for the Bidirectional Indian Sign Language Translator final-year group project at Pune Institute of Computer Technology. Sign Language Recognition (SLR) has recently emerged as a crucial technology for facilitating communication between the hearing and deaf communities owing to the advancements in Machine Learning (ML).

## Survey Scope

This paper surveys recent developments in SLR, focusing on widely appreciated and adopted techniques like Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Transformers and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. Frameworks such as MediaPipe detect hand, body, and facial landmarks with precision. Through comprehensive analysis and comparison of various approaches based on performance metrics including accuracy and Bilingual Evaluation Understudy (BLEU) scores, we identify the strengths of different architectures across static and dynamic gesture recognition techniques.

## Key Findings

The comparative analysis across CNNs, Transformers, RNN-based models, and hybrid architectures yields two key findings: MediaPipe integrated with LSTM networks demonstrates the highest accuracy for both static and dynamic gesture recognition tasks among the evaluated methods. For continuous sign language translation tasks, Hierarchical LSTM with attention mechanisms outperforms traditional LSTM and encoder-decoder frameworks at lower n-gram overlaps based on BLEU score evaluation, though performance decreases with higher-order BLEU scores due to dataset characteristics.

## Proposed Implementation

Building on previous performance and results, a real-time SLR system is presented that utilizes positional shifts in selected landmarks generated by MediaPipe to model gesture dynamics, alongside LSTM for temporal sequence modeling. The trained model then predicts gestures from live-fed video, ensuring efficient and accurate SLR.

Despite progress, challenges remain in creating universally applicable SLR systems that handle the diverse contexts and variations in sign languages. Future research should aim at enhancing model generalization by incorporating a user feedback mechanism, expanding standardized datasets, and balancing real-time performance with accessibility for both hearing and deaf communities.
